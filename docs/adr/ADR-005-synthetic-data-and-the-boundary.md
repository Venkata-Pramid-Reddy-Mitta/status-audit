# ADR-005: Seeded synthetic data, and the boundary of what the audit can establish

Status: accepted

## Context

The tool reconciles a reported RAG against a recomputed one. Two honest questions follow: where
does the data come from, and what does a flag actually prove.

## Decision: synthetic, seeded, committed

The portfolio is generated by a seeded model (`status_audit.generate`) and the CSVs are
committed. A reviewer reproduces every number with one command and no network, no API key, and
no access to a real Jira, ServiceNow or Azure DevOps instance.

## Decision: the audit reports disagreement, not truth

A flagged week means the report and the written policy disagree. It does **not** prove the
program was in trouble. The policy thresholds are a choice (ADR-002), so a flag is a statement
about the report *relative to that policy*. The sensitivity sweep prices exactly this: the
headline has a robust floor of 26 that does not move when the amber SPI line is loosened,
because those weeks are driven by open criticals, overdue milestones and red-level SPI rather
than by a marginal amber call. Tighten the amber line all the way to on-plan and the same count
inflates to 63, most of it marginal. The tool publishes the floor, not the inflated figure.

## Alternatives considered

- **Connect to a live tracker.** Out of scope by policy (convention: offline and deterministic
  by default). A live connector would make the demo realistic and the numbers irreproducible,
  which is the wrong trade for a portfolio whose entire claim is that its numbers are checkable.
  The generator models reporting *behavior* (honest, watermelon, sandbagger, rollup-liar),
  which is the part a live feed would not label anyway.
- **Claim the audit detects real trouble.** Rejected as overclaiming. The defensible claim is
  narrower and stronger: the audit detects inconsistency between a self-report and an
  independent rule, and prices how much of that inconsistency depends on the rule.

## What is therefore out of reach

Whether any flagged program was genuinely failing. Whether the policy thresholds match a given
organization's risk appetite. Both are judgement calls the tool surfaces rather than settles.
These are the subject of the README's "What this cannot measure" section.
